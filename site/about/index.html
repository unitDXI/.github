About Unit 511

Unit 511 is Fairytale.ai’s open-source training, experimentation and development group. We teach people to build AI systems by building them, and we publish everything we teach.

A builder-first philosophy#

You learn more from shipping one small thing than from reading about ten large ones. Every Unit 511 course is organised around an artifact: a notebook, a tool, a library, a document or a merged contribution. Each lesson moves that artifact forward. By the end of a course you have something real that you can run, show and build on.

We also teach in the open. Lessons are public and free, code runs on public tools, and material is licensed so anyone can reuse it with credit. When we get something wrong, anyone can open an issue or a pull request to fix it.

How it is organised#

The curriculum is organised into learning tracks. They are listed in the order most people take them:

  • Languages and tools: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, SQL, Python, Java, R, C, C++ and Git. Self-paced tracks, one per language or tool, from the first line of code. They are being written now.
  • AI Engineering. Sixteen courses in four stages:
    1. Foundations & Tooling: how AI systems work, Python, Git and open source, and ML maths.
    2. Building Systems: inference, agents, retrieval and evaluation.
    3. Applied & Responsible AI: sustainability, security, real-world domains and distributed systems.
    4. Leadership & Publishing: maintaining projects, technical writing, research and a capstone contribution.

You can work through any track or course on your own, at your own pace. In AI Engineering, cohorts work through one stage together (join a cohort). Members meet regularly, review each other’s artifacts and support one another. Cohort dates are announced on the blog and in GitHub Discussions.

Mentorship#

Cohort members are paired with mentors: experienced practitioners and graduates of earlier cohorts. Mentors review artifacts, answer questions and help people pick good first contributions. Mentoring is itself part of the curriculum. Later stages prepare you to mentor the cohorts that come after you. If you would like to mentor, see how to contribute.

Our relationship to open source#

Unit 511 depends on the open-source projects we teach with, and we aim to give back more than we take. Our rules:

  • Vendor-neutral teaching. Lessons use freely available tools and name alternatives. Nothing requires a paid account.
  • Respect for maintainers. We teach learners to follow each project’s contribution guidelines, agree changes before starting, and never send low-effort or bulk pull requests.
  • Real contributions. Capstone work lands in real projects through their normal review process. We don’t run a parallel system that bypasses it.
  • Everything reusable. Our material is licensed CC BY 4.0, so other communities can adapt it with credit.

Outcomes#

Unit 511 is new. The first cohort has not graduated yet, so we have no outcomes to report. When we do, we will publish them here, with participants’ permission: artifacts shipped, contributions merged and where people went next.

Conduct#

Everyone who takes part in Unit 511 spaces agrees to our Code of Conduct.
